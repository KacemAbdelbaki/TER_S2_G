### Main difference between a Labyrinth and a Maze (key definitions to avoid confusion later) ###

| Aspect              | Labyrinth                                                                 | Maze                                                                                         |
|---------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Meaning             | A labyrinth has a single continuous path to follow, with turns and twists but **no branches** (unicursal). | A maze is a network of paths with **branches, junctions, and dead ends**, designed to confuse. |
| Navigation          | You don’t “solve” it by choosing routes; you simply follow the path.      | You must make decisions, backtrack, and choose among alternatives to reach the goal.        |
| Difficulty          | Generally not intended to be difficult; often used for reflection/meditation. | Can be easy to extremely complex depending on layout and added constraints.                 |
| Structure           | One entrance that is also the exit; one route reaches the center (if present) and returns. | Often has distinct entrance/exit (not always), and many possible routes.                    |
| Typical generation  | Can be generated by algorithms that ensure a **single path** without cycles/branches. | Can be generated by algorithms that create **branching corridors**, loops, traps, etc.      |
